What happens to safety when AI agents work together?

AI is starting to build AI. As labs move towards recursive self-improvement, they are handing more of their research and engineering to teams of AI agents, with less and less human supervision. Most safety testing still looks at one model at a time. We build realistic research environments run by teams of agents, and measure when they collude and whether oversight catches it.

Lost in the Slop: Can AI Find the Plot in the Log?

After an incident, someone has to work out what a group of agents did and why. We wrote the history of a murder mystery by hand, let eight agents play it out, and asked frontier models to rebuild that history from the traces. The strongest monitor fully recovered less than half of it. Omissions were far more common than errors.

Predicting strategic behaviour in LLM agents

We analysed more than 200,000 decisions across game-theoretic scenarios. Models cooperate based on inferred policy correlation rather than shared identity, and a model's behaviour across sixty Prisoner's Dilemma-type settings can be partly predicted from five to ten calibration scenarios.

About us

We are a small research team in London, spun out of MATS and mentored by researchers at Google DeepMind. We study how AI agents behave when they work together. Our published work covers collusion in peer code review and persuasion attacks against the monitors that read a model's reasoning.
